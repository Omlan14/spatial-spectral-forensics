# Rehearsal script

Planned speaking slots: 110 seconds each. Ten seconds per member remain for transitions. Actual delivery must be timed aloud.

## Member 1, slide 1 (10 seconds)

We studied what simple spatial and frequency features measure when they separate real photographs from generated images. Our theme is: same score, different story.

## Member 1, slide 2 (45 seconds)

Our sample contains three thousand GenImage images: half real, half generated by BigGAN, Stable Diffusion or ADM. Real images arrive as JPEGs of different sizes; generated images arrive as fixed-size PNGs. Original format and size alone separate the classes perfectly. We standardised both classes to 256 by 256 pixels, but that enlarges BigGAN twice and reduces other images. Equal output size does not erase processing history. We therefore compare seven spatial features, seven frequency features, and all fourteen together under JPEG compression and generator shift.

## Member 1, slide 3 (55 seconds)

Our contribution is a feature-level audit. We connect image processing operations to individual statistics, generator-specific scores, and actual classification decisions. The planned study tests matched JPEG and an unseen generator. Later diagnostics examine a shared resampling operation and clean-model JPEG transfer. Those diagnostics are exploratory. BigGAN pixels are identical between the two resampling conditions, while the real-image comparison changes. However, crop and training data also change, so this is not a pure causal test of resizing. Member two will explain the pipeline and planned results.

## Member 2, slide 4 (25 seconds)

The pipeline was fixed before processing. We check duplicates and image validity, then split by image group, so variants of an image cannot leak across train and test. All planned experiments share six hundred test images. Processing is applied equally to both classes, and confidence intervals use group bootstrap resampling.

## Member 2, slide 5 (30 seconds)

Spatial features measure brightness statistics, Sobel gradients, Laplacian variation and high-pass residuals. Frequency features measure power in different bands, spectral slope, directionality and peaks. Each feature set uses the same logistic-regression recipe. Standardisation is fitted on training data; model selection and the decision threshold use validation data only. AUROC measures ranking, with one half representing chance.

## Member 2, slide 6 (25 seconds)

E1 checks the baseline. E2 deliberately mismatches compression as a shortcut control. E3 applies the same JPEG quality to both classes and refits the model at each quality. E4 holds Stable Diffusion out of training; E5 combines that holdout with JPEG quality 75. R is a later crop-and-resampling diagnostic, separate from those planned experiments.

## Member 2, slide 7 (30 seconds)

Combined features have the best pooled scores. The baseline is 0.793, but on unseen Stable Diffusion under JPEG 75 it is only 0.629, with a confidence interval from 0.571 to 0.690. Frequency alone is 0.568 and spatial alone is 0.465. Combining them helps, yet the pooled score hides near-perfect BigGAN results and weaker diffusion results. Member three will explain why that distinction matters.

## Member 3, slide 8 (40 seconds)

The exploratory diagnostic compares the same 599 test images under the original processing and a native crop followed by shared enlargement. Pooled combined AUROC changes only from 0.796 to 0.802. But BigGAN frequency AUROC falls from nearly one to 0.828, and unseen Stable Diffusion spatial AUROC rises from 0.477 to 0.771. BigGAN images themselves are unchanged. These changes show sensitivity to the processing and reference data; crop and retraining prevent us from attributing everything to resampling alone.

## Member 3, slide 9 (35 seconds)

There is a second warning. In the original pipeline frequency beats spatial features; under the diagnostic that statistically supported advantage disappears. The interval does not establish spatial superiority. JPEG also affects decisions even when ranking changes little. With the clean model and threshold fixed, spatial false positives rise from 98 to 157 out of 300 real images at quality 50. Similar AUROC therefore does not guarantee similar decisions.

## Member 3, slide 10 (25 seconds)

Our limits are one dataset and split, three generators, one held-out family and one JPEG encoder. Real and generated content is not matched. The resampling diagnostic is post-hoc and changes the crop. The next useful experiment would hold field of view fixed while comparing processing, followed by rotating the held-out generator. These are future tests, not completed results.

## Member 3, slide 11 (10 seconds)

Our conclusion: simple features separate these samples, but their interpretation depends on processing history. Report results per generator and check decisions as well as pooled scores.
